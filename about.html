About

150 km from the nearest airport to shipping AI products.

I did not grow up around engineers. I taught myself the web stack to ship a startup in college, and every job since has been a founding one.

I am from a small town in Uttar Pradesh, roughly 150 km from the nearest airport in Lucknow. Nobody around me was writing code. I taught myself the web stack in college to ship my first startup, Gangs Of Hackspur, and learned the only lesson that has stayed useful since: distribution beats product when both are weak.

Six years of engineering followed — Trilingo, then Saara, then FinSoftAI — and in each one I drifted towards the same question. Not can we build it, but should it say that at all. At FinSoftAI that became the job: a sentiment platform for retail traders where a confident wrong answer is worse than no answer, where a signal arriving after the trade is worth nothing, and where the trust layer is the product.

Alongside that I co-founded The Boring Education, a free and open-source tech education platform for India. It is now 7,000+ daily learners and a 100,000-strong community, with zero rupees spent on acquisition. I have personally mentored over a thousand students into their first engineering jobs. That community is also where my content audience came from — 144,000 people watching the process rather than the result.

So the move into AI product management is not a career change. It is the title catching up with the work: owning outcomes, sizing markets, saying no to founders, and deciding how much a model is allowed to claim.

A wrong fact delivered confidently is the product failing, not the model.
I am not a developer who wants to be a PM. I am a founder who has been doing product management without the title.
