The One Rule of Stakeholder Management
A stakeholder who disagrees with your decision is manageable. A stakeholder who disagrees and was never consulted is a crisis. Nobody with the power to block this product should ever be surprised by a decision you made.

IV How to Say No to a Founder — The Three-Step Framework
Applied to Rajniti — Real Scenario: Founder Wants to Add "Politician Social Media Tracker" Mid-Quarter
1
Acknowledge the insight, not the feature
The founder saw something real. Acknowledge the underlying observation before engaging with what they want to build.
"You're right that voters track politicians through social media more than official records. That behaviour is real and worth addressing."
2
Connect to OKRs — or show why it can't
Map their idea against the current KRs. If it connects, show how. If it does not, explain what the north star currently captures and why.
"Social media tracking doesn't move our Q1 KRs — source attribution, premium conversion, or MAU growth. It's a different user job than what we're optimising for this quarter."
3
Give it a home, not a rejection
Nobody accepts a flat no. Everyone accepts a "here is where this lives and when we revisit it." RICE it, backlog it, reference it at the next planning cycle.
"I'm adding this to the Q3 backlog with your context attached. When Saransh ships its news pipeline, social media tracking becomes a natural extension — the confidence score goes up significantly."
