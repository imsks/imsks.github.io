The Blur Test
Does showing a blurred, locked AI Performance Summary to free users increase premium conversion — or does partial visibility damage trust? One hypothesis. One primary metric. One guardrail. 14-day window.

I The Hypothesis
Full Hypothesis Statement — Committed Before Any Data Is Seen
We believe that showing a blurred, locked preview of the AI Performance Summary to free users after their third politician profile view — rather than hiding the feature entirely — will cause free-to-premium conversion rate among activated users to increase by at least 15% relative to the control, because partial visibility of a specific, valuable output creates loss aversion: users can see they are missing something concrete rather than something abstract, making the upgrade decision feel immediate rather than speculative. We will know this is true when the variant group shows a statistically significant 15%+ relative lift in conversion rate at 95% confidence over a 14-day test window, without a corresponding drop in 7-day return rate.

IV Sample Size & Duration Calculation
Three inputs determine how long this experiment runs
Baseline Conversion Rate
4.0%
Current free-to-premium rate among 3+ profile-view users. Measured over last 30 days.
Minimum Detectable Effect
15%
Relative lift we care about detecting. Below 15%, the business impact does not justify the complexity of shipping the blurred UI.
Statistical Confidence
95%
Standard threshold. 5% chance we are wrong. Power = 80% — standard for product experimentation.
~15,000 Users needed per group
~30,000 Total eligible users needed
14 days At 352,500 MAU · 35% activation rate
At 352,500 MAU with ~35% reaching 3+ profile views = ~123,000 eligible users/month = ~4,100 eligible users/day. 30,000 total users needed ÷ 4,100/day = ~7.3 days minimum. We run for 14 days to capture a full two-week behavioral cycle including weekend patterns. Do not end early even if results look conclusive after day 7.
V Experiment Timeline — 4 Phases
Phase 01
Pre-Launch Setup
Days −7 to 0
Instrument tracking events. Verify baseline metrics over 7 days. Confirm traffic split randomisation. Brief engineering on rollback plan. Set calendar reminder to check guardrail at Day 3.
Phase 02
Experiment Live
Days 1–14
Do not look at primary metric results. Check guardrail metric (7-day return rate) on Day 3 and Day 7 only. If guardrail breached, pause immediately. Avoid any other product changes during this window.
Phase 03
Analysis
Days 15–17
Run significance calculation. Check primary metric, guardrail, and both secondary metrics. Segment results by user type (new vs. returning) and device (mobile vs. web). Write results document before making ship/no-ship decision.
Phase 04
Decision & Rollout
Day 18+
Ship if primary metric hit and guardrail held. No-ship if guardrail breached or result inconclusive. Iterate if click rate was high but conversion low — pricing friction experiment next. Document learnings regardless of outcome.
VI Decision Rules — All Three Outcomes Pre-Committed
Outcome: Ship
Primary metric hit + guardrail held
Conversion rate in variant ≥ 4.6% (15%+ lift) at 95% confidence AND 7-day return rate did not drop more than 10% in the variant group.
→ Ship blurred preview to 100% of users. Start pricing optimisation experiment next.
Outcome: No-Ship
Guardrail breached or result null
7-day return rate dropped >10% in variant (trust damaged). OR primary metric showed no significant lift after 14 days. OR result was statistically significant negative (conversion declined).
→ Revert to control. Run user interviews to diagnose. Do not re-run same variant without new qualitative evidence.
Outcome: Iterate
High click rate, low conversion
Unlock button click rate >20% but conversion did not move significantly. Guardrail held. Signals the blurred preview created desire but ₹99 price point is a barrier.
→ Keep blurred preview. Run pricing experiment: ₹99 vs ₹49 trial vs free trial with card capture.
⚡
What We Are Not A/B Testing — And Why
Source attribution design is not being tested. Whether to show source links on every data point is a product values commitment, not an optimization variable. Running an A/B test on attribution would mean showing some voters unattributed political data — in a civic product, that is an ethical problem before it is a methodology question. Some decisions are made on principle, not on data. This is one of them.
