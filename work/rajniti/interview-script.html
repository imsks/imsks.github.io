A 10-question discovery script for first and second-time Indian voters. Three zones: Context → Problem → Reaction. Three probing follow-ups. No leading questions, no hypotheticals, no feature evaluation. This is how real PMs do discovery.

I Interview Setup
Target Participant
Indian voter aged 18–30. Has voted once or twice. Not a political news junkie. Lives in a constituency with a recognisable MLA/MP.
What You Are Trying to Learn
What actually happens in the week before a first-time voter casts their vote — and where the information gap creates confusion, doubt, or apathy.
What You Are NOT Doing
Testing Rajniti. Gathering feature requests. Asking for opinions. You are listening for behavior, frustration, and unmet jobs.
✓ Rules for This Interview
✓
Ask about past behavior — "tell me about the last time you..." predicts more than "would you ever..."
✓
Go one level deeper on every interesting answer — "why that specifically?" is the most useful follow-up you have
✓
Silence is productive — after a meaningful answer, wait 3 full seconds before your next question. Most real insights come in the pause.
✓
Take notes on body language, not just words — hesitation, deflection, and energy spikes are data
✗ What Kills This Interview
✗
Asking "would you use X?" — hypothetical behavior tells you nothing about actual behavior
✗
Mentioning Rajniti in Zones 1 or 2 — you contaminate the natural behavior you are trying to observe
✗
Using politically charged language — "corrupt politician", "incompetent representative" — puts participants on guard
✗
Rating scales in discovery research — you need the why, not the number
II Zone 1 — Context (10 minutes)
01
Context Zone
Build rapport. Understand the person's world before the problem. No product, no politics yet.
~10 min
1
Take me back to your first time voting. What do you remember about that day — how did you decide who to vote for?
Past behavior Memory recall Decision process
Opens with a specific, personal memory — not an opinion. Surfaces the actual decision process without priming any particular answer. If they say "I just voted for my father's party," that is the most valuable data point you can gather.
2
In the week before that election — what, if anything, did you do to learn about the candidates standing in your constituency?
Past behavior Research behavior
"If anything" is intentional — it gives permission to say "nothing." Many voters will say nothing, and that is the insight. Do not make them feel bad about it. The non-research behavior is more important to understand than the research behavior.
↳ Probe
You mentioned [what they did / didn't do] — what made that feel like enough at the time?
Listen for: "I trusted my family", "I didn't know where to look", "I didn't think it would matter." Each answer maps to a different product design implication.
3
When was the last time politics came up in a conversation with someone close to you — what were you talking about?
Past behavior Social context
Reveals how political information actually flows in this person's life. Is it family, friends, social media, news? This surfaces the informal channels Rajniti is competing with — which are almost always more trusted than any product.
III Zone 2 — The Problem (20 minutes)
02
Problem Zone
Uncover frustrations, gaps, and unmet jobs. Still no product. Go one level deeper on every interesting answer.
~20 min
4
After you voted — did you ever feel unsure whether you made the right choice? Tell me about that.
Emotional state Post-decision regret
Post-decision doubt is the emotional fingerprint of an unmet information need. If they felt confident, dig into why — that confidence may be false confidence built on limited data, which is equally interesting. If they felt doubtful, you have found the exact emotional job Rajniti solves.
↳ Probe
What specific information would have made you feel more confident — and where would you have gone to find it?
This is the highest-value question in the entire script. The answer tells you exactly what data Rajniti needs to surface and what the user's current information sources are. Write down everything verbatim.
5
Tell me about a time you heard something about a local politician — your MLA or MP — and thought "I want to know if that's actually true." What did you do next?
Verification behavior Information seeking
Tests the natural verification instinct — does it exist? How strong is it? What do they do when it fires? If the answer is "I just let it go," that is the non-consumption insight. If they went somewhere specific, that is a competitor you need to understand.
6
Has there been a moment where you felt let down by something your local representative did — or failed to do? What happened?
Accountability awareness Neutral framing
Replaces the original "have you thought your MLA is incompetent?" — which was politically charged and leading. This version asks about a felt experience without casting judgment on the politician. The participant can answer honestly without feeling they are attacking someone.
7
When the next election comes around — what is the one thing you would most want to know about the candidate standing from your area, that you currently don't know?
Information gap Future-oriented
The one exception to the "no future questions" rule — because it is about a specific information need, not a hypothetical behavior. The answer to "what would you most want to know" directly maps to Rajniti's content priorities. This question should generate your most actionable data.
↳ Probe
Why that specifically — and not [another type of information]?
Forces the participant to rank and explain their priorities. The reasoning behind their top information need reveals the emotional job underneath it — usually accountability, trust, or relevance to their own life.
IV Zone 3 — Reaction (10 minutes)
03
Reaction Zone
Introduce the concept only — not a demo. Watch for recognition or confusion. No feature evaluation, no rating scales.
~10 min
8
I want to describe something to you and get your honest reaction — not whether you think it's a good idea, just whether it connects to anything you've just been telling me. Imagine a tool that shows you your MLA's attendance record, how they voted on bills in your state, and an AI-generated summary of their track record — all from official government sources. What's your first reaction?
Concept reaction Not a feature ask
Introduces the Rajniti concept as a concept, not a product. "What's your first reaction?" is deliberately open. Watch for leaning forward (recognition of a felt need) vs. polite nodding (it sounds nice but disconnected from their life). These are completely different signals.
9
Is there anything about what I just described that would make you trust it — or not trust it?
Trust signals Credibility barriers
The most important Rajniti-specific question in the script. Civic tech lives or dies on trust. The answer will surface exactly what source attribution language, what institutional credibility signals, and what design patterns Rajniti needs to establish trust with this specific user segment.
V Closing — NPS Proxy + Open Space
Question 10 — NPS Proxy (Keep at End, Always)
If you knew a friend who was trying to figure out who to vote for in the next election — how likely would you be to mention something like what I described today? What would you say about it?
This is the NPS question restructured for discovery interviews. "How likely" gives the signal. "What would you say about it" gives you the verbatim language users would naturally use to describe Rajniti to someone else — which is your organic marketing copy, unfiltered.
Open Space — Always End With This
Is there anything you expected me to ask that I didn't — or anything you think is important that we haven't covered?
The open space question surfaces the blind spots in your script. If three participants in a row bring up the same thing you never asked about — you have discovered an insight your research design missed. This question is how you fix your next interview.
VI After the Interview — What to Listen For in the Recording
Signals That Validate the Product
Post-vote doubt expressed unprompted
Tried to verify information and couldn't find it
Leaning forward when the concept was introduced
Describes the information gap in specific terms
Would share it with a specific person in mind
Signals That Challenge Assumptions
"I trust my family's judgment more than any app"
Confident about their vote despite low information
Never experienced post-vote doubt
Already uses another source that satisfies the job
Skeptical of AI-generated summaries specifically
Trust Architecture Signals
Names a specific credible institution unprompted
Asks "where does the data come from?"
Mentions WhatsApp forwards as a negative example
Uses the word "unbiased" or "neutral" spontaneously
Distinguishes between opinion and record-based facts
